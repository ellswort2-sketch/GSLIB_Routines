Omnid fits one curve. Direct fits all loaded curves jointly, sharing nugget and model coefficients while allowing a separate range for each structure and direction. Remove omnidirectional rows from the CSV before a joint directional fit if they should not be included.

2 · Model coefficients and constraints

Coefficients are partial sills, except the power coefficient. Total sill is nugget plus the enabled bounded-model coefficients. A power component has no finite sill. Disabled terms contribute zero.

Range/exponent bounds apply when its Fit box is checked. Set equal bounds to hold a parameter fixed. Power entries are exponents (0 < exponent ≤ 2), not distance ranges. Freeze unnecessary terms before fitting.

Model equations and workbook differences

γ(h) = nugget + Σ coefficient × structure(h), for h > 0; γ(0) = 0.

Spherical: 1.5(h/a) − 0.5(h/a)³ for h < a, then 1.
Exponential: 1 − exp(−3h/a).
Gaussian: 1 − exp(−k(h/a)²), k = 3 for the workbook or 9 for this KT3D.
Power: h^exponent.

To preserve a Gaussian curve, switching from workbook to KT3D multiplies each Gaussian range by √3. The selector performs this conversion automatically. The workbook Direct objective references are disconnected from its model parameters; this page connects each curve to its directional ranges. The original workbook divides weights by their sum over its first 14 fitted rows. The comparison button restores that scaling and the original 499-row example. Normal fitting uses the selected rows consistently.

3 · Fitting settings

Near-zero bins at a positive mean distance can be included. Inverse-γ² weights require positive γ. Pair-based weights require positive pair counts. Unknown counts remain available with uniform or inverse-γ² weights; filter details appear in the results.

Bounded fitting uses multiple deterministic starting points. Inspect the result and try simpler models and alternative starting values. A local minimum or a parameter at a bound can indicate that more than one model explains the data.

Export treats the selected curve as isotropic; a joint directional fit does not by itself define a valid anisotropic ellipsoid. Gaussian ranges are converted to this KT3D’s convention. Power export is disabled because the supplied COVA3’s nested-power covariance differs from the workbook’s additive power formulation.
